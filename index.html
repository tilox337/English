Connection to Group

A group is what you obtain when associativity, identity, and invertibility (equivalently, divisibility) hold together.

Axiom matrix

What each name actually requires

Invertibility is stated for monoids (every element has a two-sided inverse). Divisibility is stated for magmas (left and right multiplication are bijective). In a group the two conditions coincide.

The four arrows

Properties that change the name

01

Associativity

The operation may be composed without regard to parentheses: (a · b) · c = a · (b · c) for all elements a, b, c. Associativity turns a magma into a semigroup, a quasigroup into an associative quasigroup, a unital magma into a monoid, and a loop into a group.

02

Identity

There exists an element e such that e · a = a · e = a for every a. Identity turns a magma into a unital magma, a semigroup into a monoid, a quasigroup into a loop, and an associative quasigroup into a group.

03

Divisibility

Equations a · x = b and y · a = b have unique solutions x, y for every pair a, b. Equivalently, every left and right multiplication map is bijective — the Latin-square property. Divisibility turns a magma into a quasigroup, a unital magma into a loop, and a semigroup into an associative quasigroup.

04

Invertibility

Once an identity e is present, every element a is asked to have a two-sided inverse a−1 with a · a−1 = a−1 · a = e. Invertibility is the arrow from monoid to group. In that setting it supplies unique divisibility as well.
